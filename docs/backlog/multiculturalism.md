## Slide 1 — Official since 1971

_General framing: the policy, then the size of the thing._

- Canada was the **first country in the world** to make multiculturalism official policy: Pierre Trudeau, House of Commons, **October 8, 1971**.
- Parliament went further in **1988** with the Canadian Multiculturalism Act, the first national multiculturalism law anywhere.
- **23% of Canadians** were born abroad in the 2021 census: the highest share since Confederation, and the highest in the G7.
- That census counted more than **450 ethnic or cultural origins** and over **200 mother tongues**. Nearly half of Toronto was born outside the country.

**Images**: Trudeau in the Commons, 1971; a citizenship ceremony; a Canada Day crowd; a
census-style map or infographic. _Difficulty: medium; the 1971 statement itself has little
free imagery._

## Slide 2 — Tile by tile

_Zoom in: whole neighbourhoods built by one community._

- Most big cities in Canada have neighborhoods dedicated to different cultures. For example, in Vancouver, there's Chinatown, Little Italy, Greektown, Little India, etc.
- Yiddish was **Montreal's third language** by 1931, spoken end to end along Saint-Laurent Boulevard, still called The Main.
- **Richmond**, next door to Vancouver, is **60% foreign-born**, the highest share of any city in Canada.
- **1.36 million** Canadians report Ukrainian roots, third only to Ukraine and Russia.

**Images**: Saint-Laurent Boulevard storefronts or a Yiddish-signed shop; the Sam Kee
Building end-on; the Millennium Gate on Pender; Richmond's Golden Village at night; the
Vegreville egg; Punjabi Market on Main Street. _Difficulty: easy._

## Slide 3 — Cooked up in Canada

_Zoom in further: the food that came out of it._

- **Hawaiian pizza** was invented in **Chatham, Ontario, 1962**, by Greek immigrant Sam Panopoulos. So a Greek guy (Greece) invented a pizza (Italian) with pineapple (Hawaiian) in Canada.
- **Ginger beef** is Calgarian, not Chinese: chef George Wong battered it at the Silver Inn in **1975** for Albertans who wanted beef crisp, not chewy.
- Montreal bagels are hand-rolled, boiled in **honey water**, and wood-fired. Fairmount has been turning them out since **1919**.
- Vancouver chef **Hidekazu Tojo** says he flipped the sushi roll rice-side out in the 1970s, for diners who wanted the seaweed hidden. He _also_ made the California roll.

**Images**: pineapple pizza; ginger beef; a wood-fired bagel oven; sushi rolls; a Punjabi
Market sweet shop; a night-market food stall. _Difficulty: easy._
