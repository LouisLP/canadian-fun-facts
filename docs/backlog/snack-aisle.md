## Slides (draft copy)

### 1. All dressed up — chips

- **All-dressed** packs four flavours onto one chip: ketchup, BBQ, sour cream and onion, salt
  and vinegar.
- The name started on pizza (**toute garnie** in French: the works); Quebec's **Yum Yum** had
  a chip version by **1978**.
- **Ketchup chips** were mainly a **Hostess** product through the 1970s and 80s, before
  Frito-Lay bought the brand.

### 2. Bar none — chocolate

- Our **Smarties** are chocolate; America's chalky candy rolls with the same name are sold
  here as **Rockets**.
- **Coffee Crisp** dates to **1938** and is made at Nestlé's Toronto factory.
- **Caramilk** launched in **1968**, and its ads spent about **40 years** teasing how the
  caramel gets inside.

### 3. It's in the bag — Kraft Dinner and bagged milk

- Canadians buy about **1.7 million** of the 7 million boxes of **Kraft Dinner** sold worldwide
  each week.
- A 4-litre bag of milk holds three **1⅓-litre** pouches; drop one in a pitcher and snip the
  corner.
- Metric conversion helped bags stick, since moving the cut was easier than redesigning jugs;
  they've since vanished from **BC and Alberta**.

## Check before publishing

- **KD figure:** about 1.7 million traces to around 2008, and the sources share one origin.
  The UBC dictionary entry couldn't be opened, so its figures came from a search summary.
- **Yum Yum:** not proven to be first, so the copy says "by 1978".
- **Coffee Crisp:** Wikipedia says 1938, others say 1939, and the Wikipedia page is flagged as
  needing more citations.
- **Caramilk:** "about 40 years" comes from trade press on the campaign.
- **Not claimed:** "bars Americans can't get", because Coffee Crisp has had some US sales.
  Smarties and Coffee Crisp both trace back to the British firm Rowntree's, so neither is
  called a Canadian invention.
