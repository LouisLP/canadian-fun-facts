## Slides (draft copy, numbers verified)

### 1. A walk in the park — Stanley Park and the Seawall

- Stanley Park covers **405 hectares**, about a fifth bigger than New York's Central Park
  (341 ha).
- The Seawall took **63 years** to finish, from 1917 to 1980.
- Its **28 km** waterfront path, Convention Centre to Spanish Banks, is the world's longest
  uninterrupted one.
- A December 2006 windstorm flattened roughly **10,000 trees** in the park in a single
  night.

### 2. Peak performance — the North Shore

- The **Grouse Grind**: 2,830 steps and about 800 m of climbing, locals' "Mother Nature's
  Stairmaster".
- The first **Capilano Suspension Bridge** (1889) was hemp rope and cedar planks; today's
  spans about **140 m**, 70 m above the river.
- Three ski hills, **Grouse, Cypress and Seymour**, sit across the harbour from downtown;
  Cypress hosted 2010 Olympic snowboarding and freestyle.

### 3. Up from the ashes — Gastown, the fire, the clock

- It started with **Gassy Jack** Deighton, a barrel of whisky and a saloon in 1867;
  Gastown is named after him.
- The CPR's boss renamed the town from Granville to **Vancouver**; it incorporated on
  **6 April 1886**.
- Two months later, on **13 June 1886**, a land-clearing fire burned the brand-new city
  almost to the ground.
- The Victorian-looking **steam clock** in Gastown was built in **1977**, partly to cover a
  vent in the downtown steam-heating network.

### 4. World's fair game — Expo 86

- **Expo 86** drew about **22 million** visits in under six months.
- **Canada Place**, with its white sails, started life as the fair's Canada Pavilion.
- SkyTrain opened in December 1985 to carry the fair's crowds. (Keep it to this; travel set
  already covers SkyTrain stats.)
- The fair lost about **$311 million**, but left behind landmarks still in use.

### Alternate slide: Raincouver

- Vancouver gets about **1,199 mm** of rain a year, less than Halifax (1,452 mm) or
  St. John's (1,514 mm) (StatCan 1971–2000 normals).
- The reputation comes from the grey, drizzly months, November to March.
